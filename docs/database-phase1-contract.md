## 2. Non-negotiable boundaries

1. Keep one Molecular Database, one Evaluation system, and the existing scheduler and replication architecture. Do not create parallel stores.
2. Keep the Database a curated scientific library, not a mirror of every Results job, candidate, intermediate, or docking pose.
3. Preserve full Results and their artifacts. Library retention or indexing changes must not delete, rewrite, or relocate Results evidence.
4. pc002975, PC004032, and PC004033 each author and ingest locally, including while another workstation is offline. Existing owner-authoritative replication converges those local records; pc002975 is not a central ingestion service.
5. Canonical identities may deduplicate across peers. Different structures, poses, source records, provenance, and Evaluations remain distinct and attributable to their actual authors.
6. Use only isolated temporary databases, fixtures, and simulated peer transports for implementation verification.
7. Do not modify production Database rows or files; rebuild or bulk-import any production Database; synchronize production peers; restart services; deploy; push; or change production replication state. Phase 2 is out of scope.
8. Do not invent acceptance, coordinates, experimental/native status, target correspondence, or missing scientific measurements. Preserve missing and unknown as such.

## 4. Scientific inclusion and representation rules

### Molecule identities

- Protein and nucleic-acid identity follows defined component composition/sequence under the existing identity contract. A target is a role, not automatically another molecule.
- Small-molecule identity preserves stereochemistry and chemically meaningful distinctions using existing canonical chemistry support.
- Chain labels, binder/target roles, pose identifiers, model identifiers, and run identifiers describe a representation or observation; they do not create a duplicate molecule identity by themselves.
- A multi-component complex retains its component relationships. A binder may remain the reusable identity while a target–binder complex is a separate representation connected to the exact modeled target.
- Sequence-only proteins are valid. Do not fabricate coordinates or assert a structural reference.

### Structures and docking associations

For every curated molecule, retain or associate scientifically valid source structures when they are available:

- **Proteins/targets:** original supplied PDB/mmCIF and templates, with source chain/sequence relationship; experimental coordinates only where evidence supports that claim; separately identified predicted structures.
- **Designed binders:** individual binder structures when supplied and complete target–binder complexes when available, with target/binder chain mapping and acceptance-policy provenance.
- **Small molecules:** original chemical identity and coordinate-bearing source SDF when available; retain original input and prepared state separately.
- **Docking:** original ligand SDF, prepared ligand state, selected pose (preferably exportable as SDF), exact receptor PDB/mmCIF, original PDBQT where relevant, score/run/method/pose identifiers.
- **Protein–ligand complexes:** complete bound complex linked to the exact receptor structure and ligand pose used to build it.
- **Historical source:** source archive/files and checksums, native model/pose and chain/atom mappings, IDs, metadata, metrics, and provenance.

Distinguish supplied/original, experimental, prepared, predicted, and reconstructed/exported representations. A valid structure relationship identifies both the molecular identity and exact representation, not just a matching sequence. For unavailable remote content, record whether the file is locally available, remotely retrievable from its authorized owner, or unavailable; preserve checksums in each case.

Reuse Studio’s existing bound-docking and structure-export infrastructure. Do not add a second structure store. Reject ambiguous PDBQT-to-SDF reconstruction; never infer atom order from an unrelated canonical SMILES.
